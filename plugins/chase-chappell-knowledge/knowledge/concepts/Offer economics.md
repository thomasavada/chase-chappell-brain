# Offer economics

Watch: [I Studied Every Ecommerce Offer So You Don’t Have To](https://youtu.be/RHZ39WfEuM0)

An offer should exchange **unit margin for a measurable downstream event**, not merely lower price:

| Trade | Use | Economic job |
|---|---|---|
| Margin → conversion | Inventory speed, free gift, shipping, email capture | Make “yes” easier only when the acquired action is worth the cost |
| Margin → larger cart | Multipacks, volume tiers, spend thresholds, order bumps | Raise AOV and reopen acquisition margin |
| Margin → repeat purchase | Subscription, prepaid supply, second-order incentive | Accept weaker order-one economics to raise LTV and allowable CAC |

## Decision rule

Model the chain before launching: **offer → CVR/AOV/LTV → CAC ceiling → contribution margin → cash timing**. A higher ROAS or CVR can still lose more money when the brand absorbs shipping or discounts a low-value single unit. Prefer structures where the customer gets a better unit deal while the brand receives a larger cart or longer relationship.

## Ladder

1. Take the low-risk gains first: one-click upsells, order bumps, gift-with-purchase, and relevant cross-sells.
2. Use multipacks and volume thresholds when the brand needs profitable first-order scale.
3. Use subscription/prepaid offers only after cohort retention, contribution margin, and payback period are known.
4. Avoid naked hero-SKU discounts, free shipping on uneconomic low-AOV orders, and BOGO unless unit economics support them.

## Capture offer

Chase’s current preference is a full-width, interactive **mystery offer** rather than a generic “join our list” box. He claims roughly **15–18% opt-in** across examples, versus lower rates for no offer or a plain 10%-off popup. Treat this as a claimed benchmark and test it against margin and list quality. Capture operations remain in [[Email and SMS]].

## Cash-flow bound

A subscription brand can deliberately lose on order one because later orders raise LTV and its auction ceiling. That is not permission to spend to a forecast: a small brand can run out of cash before payback arrives. Bundle-first economics are safer when working capital is constrained. ## Seasonal offer ladder

BFCM is a sequence, not one discount stretched across a month: test gift-with-purchase, spend thresholds and bundles in October; give VIP/email/SMS a smaller early-access offer; move into the public main sale; reserve the deepest hero-SKU deal for the peak; then switch to gifting bundles and shipping-deadline angles through December. Change ads, site banners and lifecycle messages with every stage. Early offers should maximize AOV and create a retargeting/list pool; do not burn the deepest discount first.
